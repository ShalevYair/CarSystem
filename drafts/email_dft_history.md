**Subject:** Historical derivation of in-service exhaust emission default limits (Construction & Use Regulations / MOT)

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing from the Israeli Ministry of Transport. We are updating the statutory default pass/fail values used in periodic emission testing at our vehicle inspection centres, and our regulatory model closely follows the British one: a manufacturer-specified value where available, and a generic default limit by engine age and fuel type otherwise. Our measurement method (idle / fast-idle CO, HC and lambda) is methodologically identical to the MOT metered test.

Simon Smith, Product Manager of the MOT Testing Service at DVSA, has kindly confirmed the current operational model in correspondence with us (June 2026), and referred us to the Department for Transport for the historical and analytical background. Specifically, we would appreciate any information or archived documentation on:

1. **Derivation of the default limits.** How were the generic default limits in the Road Vehicles (Construction and Use) Regulations originally derived (e.g., 3.5% CO for pre-1986 vehicles, 0.3% CO / lambda 0.97–1.03 for post-2002 catalyst vehicles)? Was the derivation based on fleet measurement data, type-approval margins, engineering judgment, or a combination?
2. **Threshold-setting for the per-model annex.** For the In-Service Exhaust Emission Standards booklet (19th edition), what analytical procedure was used to position each per-model limit between known-good and known-defective vehicles?
3. **Impact assessments.** Are there published or archived impact assessments / consultation documents from past revisions of the emission limits (e.g., the May 2018 diesel changes) that describe the evidence base?

We would be glad to share our own fleet-based derivation methodology in return, and are available for a call if more convenient.
