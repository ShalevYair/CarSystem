**Subject:** Follow-up: Statistical methodology behind Smog Check cutpoint derivation

Dear Mr. Hedglin,

Thank you again for your detailed reply earlier this month regarding the California Smog Check program and the applicability limits of the VLT cutpoints. Your warning about fleet-specific statistical derivation was well taken, and it has directly shaped our direction: rather than adopting external tables, we now plan to derive generic pass/fail default values from measurements of the Israeli fleet itself, collected at our licensing test facilities.

Since BAR pioneered exactly this kind of fleet-based statistical derivation, and since you kindly invited further questions, I would be grateful for your guidance on the methodology itself:

1. **Percentile selection.** When the original cutpoints were derived from fleet emission distributions, what percentile (or failure-rate target) was used to set the initial pass/fail threshold, and how was it chosen?
2. **Stratification.** How was the fleet segmented for derivation (model year groups, engine displacement, vehicle weight class, technology group)? What minimum sample size per group was considered statistically sufficient?
3. **Separating wear from malfunction.** Did BAR use known-defective vs. known-good vehicle samples (e.g., before/after repair data) to position the threshold, or purely distributional statistics?
4. **Calibration over time.** We understand cutpoints were adjusted by up to roughly ±30% based on observed failure rates. What monitoring informed those adjustments, and at what cadence were they made?
5. **Documentation.** Is there a public (or shareable) technical report documenting the original cutpoint derivation methodology that we could cite in our regulatory work?

Any pointers — including to former colleagues or archived reports — would be greatly appreciated. We are of course happy to share our resulting methodology in return.
